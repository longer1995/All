# ALL — Go-to-Market Strategy & Build Plan

> Synthesis of research rounds 1–3 (docs 01–09), 2026-08-07. This is the operating plan: positioning, channels, sequencing, and the concrete list of what to change and build for GTM success. Where a claim traces to research, the source doc is cited as (0X).

## 1. Positioning: what ALL is and says

**One-liner:** *The modern FFL platform that keeps your license safe — bound book, 4473, and NICS in one flow, with a migration so careful it's audit-verified, and no integration tax.*

Positioning pillars, each grounded in a documented market gap:

1. **Audit-defense first.** Fear of ATF revocation is the category's #1 purchase trigger — every incumbent's sharpest asset is a revocation-averted testimonial or guarantee (07). ALL sells like insurance, not productivity software: lead with inspection outcomes, violation-prevention stats (7 of ATF's top-10 violations are 4473 errors — 03), and a funded compliance/legal-defense guarantee at every tier (parity with FastBound/Orchid, 06).
2. **The painless, audit-verified switch.** Switching is the moat protecting incumbents; migration terror is rational (a transcription error is a federal violation) (07, 09). ALL productizes white-glove, audit-verified A&D/4473 migration with an accuracy guarantee — positioned as *more* compliance-rigorous than staying put. BoundPro already validated migration-importers as the attack wedge (01).
3. **No integration tax, no lock-in.** Gearfire's documented fee-tripling dispute is a citable grievance (07). ALL commits publicly: zero third-party integration fees, full data export any time. Defensive requirement and offensive marketing in one.
4. **Your data is not a registry.** Registry fear is substantiated (NSSF/Cambridge Analytica precedent; MCC tracking fight) (08). Publish a plain-language data policy: never shared beyond legally mandated ATF reporting, never sold, never political — and architect it truthfully (per-FFL custody, no cross-dealer transaction search; FOPA constraints, 03).
5. **Built by an operator, not a tourist.** Insider credibility is the trust shortcut every competitor's agency manufactures (08, 09). Brandon's history — co-founded and scaled Ammunation to ~$4.8M/75+ accounts, built GCDL under ATF FEL/ITAR/DCMA oversight, held FFL/FEL licenses, closed Honeywell and Nobel Sport deals — is the genuine article. It leads the website, the sales deck, and every first call.

**Pricing posture:** transparent flat tiers on a public pricing page (opacity is the category norm and a resented one — 01): compliance wedge ~$29–79/mo, POS bundle ~$99–199/mo, payments quoted with contracted acquirers only. No usage-based pricing (FastBound's most-resented trait), no contracts, free file copies. Keep a real SaaS floor — do not go $0-software/payments-only (Dutchie lesson, 09).

## 2. Target and beachhead

- **ICP:** independent retail gun shops and ranges-with-retail, 5–50 employees, single-to-few locations, ~$500K–$5M revenue (02). Not kitchen-table FFLs, not big chains, initially not pure manufacturers.
- **Beachhead: Texas, Houston-first.** ~5,200 Type-01 dealers statewide, ~1 in 10 nationally (02); Brandon's home market and relationship base; the Toast metro-density playbook maps directly — anchor accounts become show-floor references for dealers who already visit each other for consignment and wholesale (09).
- Expansion path: Houston → DFW/SA/Austin → FL/PA/OH (confirm by dealer-density data) → regional multi-location chains as the upmarket move (02).
- Planning discipline: model years 1–3 at low single-digit penetration (Shopmonkey: 2% after 8 years, well-funded — 09). 300 paying dealers at blended $150/mo ≈ $540K ARR before payments; payments attach is where the curve bends (04).

## 4. What we need to CHANGE (deltas to current thinking)

1. **Payments moves later in the sequence, earlier in the contracts.** The revenue model (04) is right, but GTM-wise: no payments at 0→10 customers; sign **two** high-risk acquirers in writing before quoting any dealer a rate (Dutchie's single-processor collapse is the cautionary tale) (09).
2. **The migration offer is the product's sharpest GTM feature, not an ops afterthought.** Elevate "audit-verified migration with accuracy guarantee" to headline status alongside the compliance guarantee — it's what converts an incumbent's customer, and the whole market's 4473 layer is in regulatory flux right now, the cheapest switching moment in a decade (01, 05).
3. **Add the two guarantees to the product plan** (they were absent from the v1 scope in 05): a funded legal-defense/compliance guarantee (FastBound/Orchid parity — table stakes for the fear-driven buyer) and the migration accuracy guarantee. Budget for the legal-defense partnership (FFLGuard-style) as a COGS line.
4. **Trust surfaces are launch-blocking, not polish**: the no-registry data policy page, founder story, named advisors (ideally an ex-ATF/IOI or FFL attorney on the advisory board), and the zero-integration-fee pledge all ship with the website, before the first outbound call (08).
5. **Kill any residual plan for**: cold email as a primary channel (SHAFT), Meta ads, $0-software pricing, native mobile apps for commerce, and expecting distributor co-marketing (07, 08).
6. **Texas-first, not national-first**: all round-2 thinking was segment-scoped but geography-agnostic; the density/referral evidence says pick Houston and win it before spending a dollar elsewhere (09).

## 5. What we need to BUILD (GTM-driven roadmap)

### Phase A — Pre-revenue (months 0–3), alongside core product v1 (05)
- **Migration engine + service playbook**: importers for FastBound, Orchid eBound, Easy Bound Book exports *and paper-book digitization* (the biggest untapped pool is still paper — 02); dual-verification workflow (OCR + human check) producing an audit-verification report the dealer keeps. This is both onboarding tooling and the sales pitch.
- **Trust stack**: public pricing page, no-registry data policy, founder/advisor pages, zero-integration-fee pledge, compliance + migration guarantees with real legal backing.
- **SEO foundation**: ATF inspection-prep checklist (gated), 4473 error-code reference, bound-book explainers, and vs-pages. 6–12 month payoff clock starts now (08).
- **Outbound engine**: ATF list → Type 01/02 filter → GBP enrichment (phone, reviews, website) → Houston-metro call list with weekday-morning cadence; direct-mail piece for the no-digital-presence tail (08).
- **Demo-ready "Inspection Mode" prototype** (06 Tier-2 #6): the guided IOI-walkthrough is the single most demo-able differentiator for a fear-driven buyer — build a credible version early even if v1-thin.

### Phase B — 0→10 customers (months 3–9): founder-led Houston
- Founder-supervised white-glove migration for every account; treat each as embedded research (Squire's barbershop lesson — 09).
- Compliance wedge only, priced (~$29–79/mo), 14-day self-serve trial available but sales-led in practice.
- Sign the first ISO + insurance + consultant referral partnerships; join FFL Consultants' partner roster (08).
- Attend Texas association events and NSSF RRB Expo as attendees/relationship-builders.
- Instrument real churn and time-to-value from day one; separate "business closed" churn from product churn (09).

### Phase C — 10→100 (months 9–24): densify Texas, add POS + payments
- Ship POS tied to auto-disposition (the zero-double-entry wedge — 05); AI 4473 error-checking and serial OCR (06).
- Contract 2 high-risk acquirers; launch embedded payments to existing happy customers first (blended Boulevard/Shopmonkey model — 09).
- Launch the formal incentivized referral program; first paid case studies/testimonials in the revocation-averted genre (07).
- Pitch NSSF Affinity Partnership and one buying group (NBS committee) with live references in hand (07).
- First SHOT Show booth (Jan cycle) once ≥25–50 referenceable customers.
- One deep distributor integration (RSR or Sports South) for parity credibility (05).

### Phase D — 100→1000 (months 24+): field team + multi-state
- Metro-territory field/inside hybrid reps ("mayor of the patch") in DFW/SA/Austin, then FL/PA/OH (09).
- State compliance modules (CA DES, IL FTIP, NY reporting, WA/CO permit regimes) as the expansion product — the Flowhub fragmented-compliance play, adapted (03, 09).
- Buying-group/NSSF preferred-vendor endorsement formalized; incumbent-displacement motion (migration concierge + termination-fee absorption for multi-location dealers) aimed at Bravo/FastBound bases (07, 09).
- Attach layer: Credova-style BNPL referral, insurance referral, transfer-network fees once GMV justifies (04).

## 6. GTM budget shape (directional)

- **Cheap and high-leverage**: ATF list + GBP enrichment (~free), SEO content (time), partner rev-shares (success-based), NSSF membership (~$100–750/yr), RRB Expo (low four figures).
- **Moderate**: SHOT booth (~$5–15K all-in), migration service labor (COGS per account — this is the CAC, spend it happily), legal-defense guarantee partnership.
- **Avoid until proven**: paid search (pre-clear policy with Google first — 08), any Meta spend, field-sales hires before 100 customers (09).

## 7. Metrics that decide the plan is working

- 0→10: migration NPS ≥ 9; every account willing to be a named reference; time-to-first-compliant-entry < 1 day.
- 10→100: ≥20% of new signups referral-sourced (Toast benchmark); monthly logo churn < 2% excluding business closures; CAC payback < 12 months on SaaS alone.
- 100+: payments attach ≥ 60% of eligible dealers; payments gross profit ≥ SaaS gross profit trajectory (Toast/Shopmonkey pattern); at least one association/buying-group endorsement signed.
