# US FFL Customer Base — Market Research Report

> Deep-research output, 2026-08-06. Sources are linked inline; figures flagged as unverified should be re-checked against ATF primary PDFs before use in a pitch deck.

## 1. Market Size

**Total active FFLs (Types 01/02/03/06/07/08/09/10/11 combined):** approximately **128,690–132,000** as of FY2023–FY2024, down from a 2013 peak of **139,244**. [ATF FY2024 Fact Sheet](https://www.atf.gov/resource-center/fact-sheet/facts-and-figures-fiscal-year-2024) via [FastBound](https://www.fastbound.com/ffl-blog/2024-atf-firearms-commerce-report-key-trends-every-ffl-needs-to-know/). ATF also publishes live state-by-state license counts quarterly: [Active Firearms Licenses by State — July 2025](https://www.atf.gov/media/28391/download), [May 2025](https://www.atf.gov/media/28381/download), [January 2025](https://www.atf.gov/media/28361/download). A separate market study cites **51,272 gun dealers** (narrower "dealer" definition) mapped by state — [PRWeb/study release](https://www.prweb.com/releases/new-data-study-maps-where-americas-51-272-gun-dealers-are-wyoming-leads-at-38x-new-jerseys-rate-302840163.html).

**By license type (most recent figures found; consolidated national total-by-type table not fully available via search — recommend pulling directly from ATF's [Firearms Commerce annual report](https://www.atf.gov/media/22706/download) PDF for exact FY2024/25 splits):**

| Type | Description | ~2013 | ~2021 | ~2025 | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Dealer in firearms other than destructive devices | 54,026 | 52,993 | **46,072** | Declining every year since 2016 peak (56,074) |
| 02 | Pawnbroker dealing in firearms | — | — | subset of totals above | Roughly stable, small share |
| 03 | Collector of curios & relics (C&R) | — | — | not found in search (ATF report needed) | Historically large (~2nd biggest category after 01/07), $30/3yr, no storefront required |
| 06 | Manufacturer of ammunition (not firearms) | — | — | — | growing modestly |
| 07 | Manufacturer of firearms other than destructive devices | 9,094 | — | **20,023** (2023) | More than doubled 2013→2023 |

Sources: [American Rifleman — "Number of Retail FFLs Dwindling"](https://www.americanrifleman.org/content/number-of-retail-ffls-dwindling/), [Gun Industry Accountability — "America's Hidden Gun Manufacturers"](https://gunindustryaccountability.org/issue/americas-hidden-gun-manufacturers/), [ATF Fact Sheet: FFL Types](https://www.atf.gov/resource-center/fact-sheet/2024-federal-firearms-and-explosives-licenses-types).

**10-year trend narrative:** The 2016 ATF fee restructuring made Type 07 manufacturer licenses cheap relative to their expanded privileges (manufacture + deal), pulling volume away from Type 01. Small storefront dealers can't match the SKU breadth or margin of manufacturer-direct and big-box channels, so **Type 01 count has fallen ~18% since 2016** while **Type 07 has more than doubled**, and overall gun sales/NICS volume kept climbing — consolidation into fewer, larger points of sale. [American Rifleman](https://www.americanrifleman.org/content/number-of-retail-ffls-dwindling/).

**Geographic distribution:** Texas, Florida, and Pennsylvania lead in absolute FFL count; **Texas alone ≈ 5,224 dealers (Type 01), ~1 in 10 nationwide**. California has 11,838 total active FFLs across all types (largest state by raw count due to population, but low per-capita density). Per-capita density is inverted from population: **Wyoming leads at 86.4 dealers per 100,000 residents** (~38x New Jersey's rate); national average ≈15 per 100,000; lowest densities: **Washington D.C. (0.6), New Jersey (2.3), California (3.4), Massachusetts (3.7), Hawaii (6.0)**. Sources: [Orchid LLC — All 50 States Ranked by FFLs](https://orchidadvisors.com/all-50-states-ranked-by-number-of-ffls/), [PRWeb dealer density study](https://www.prweb.com/releases/new-data-study-maps-where-americas-51-272-gun-dealers-are-wyoming-leads-at-38x-new-jerseys-rate-302840163.html), [ATF state reports](https://www.atf.gov/media/28391/download).

## 2. Segment Profiles

| Segment | Est. count / share | Typical revenue | Staffing | Tech sophistication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kitchen-table / home-based FFL** | ~57% of licensed addresses residential per 2022 ATF data (**40,831 residential vs 30,914 commercial**); historically 60–80% in earlier decades | Often side-income, low-volume (transfers, C&R sales, occasional dealer sales) | Owner-operator, no employees | Very low — paper bound books common, minimal/no POS, price-sensitive |
| **Small independent retail gun shop** | Bulk of the ~46,000 Type 01s not home-based | Low six figures to ~$1–2M | 2–8 staff | Low-moderate — many still paper, some basic POS (Bravo, AIM, RTG) |
| **Range with retail attached** | Subset of Type 01/07, thousands nationwide, hard to isolate exact count | $1M+ (range fees + retail), higher fixed costs | 5–20+ (range safety officers, retail, gunsmiths) | Moderate — need booking/POS integration, growing digital adoption |
| **Pawn shops with FFL (Type 02)** | Subset of Type 01/02 count, thousands | Firearms often minority of pawn revenue | Existing pawn staff, not firearms-specialist | Moderate — already run pawn-industry software, firearms compliance often bolted on |
| **Large chains** (Academy, Bass Pro/Cabela's, Sportsman's Warehouse) | Academy: 300+ stores; **Bass Pro + Cabela's combined: 169 stores** (Cabela's-branded ≈49 as of April 2025); Sportsman's Warehouse: ~100+ | $100M+ per chain; billions combined | Hundreds to thousands of employees | High — enterprise POS/ERP, compliance teams, likely build custom or use enterprise vendors |
| **Type 07 manufacturers** | **20,023** (2023), more than doubled since 2013; includes many one-person "manufacturer-dealers" using the license for lower fees/broader privileges | Wide range — solo hobbyist to large OEM | 1 to 1,000+ | Bimodal — small ones low-tech, large ones need production/inventory/compliance systems |
| **Gunsmiths** | Subset of Type 01/07 holders; no isolated ATF count found | Service-revenue heavy | 1–5 | Low — service/repair tracking needs, compliance recordkeeping for work-in |
| **Online-only / dropship-to-FFL dealers** | Growing but uncounted segment (GunBroker, Guns.com marketplace sellers, PSA online) riding on partner FFLs for transfer | Can be large | Varies widely | High for major platforms, moderate for small online resellers |

Sources: [The Trace — Kitchen-Table Dealers](https://www.thetrace.org/2021/05/atf-inspections-kitchen-table-home-based-ffl/), [VPC — No Deal: Kitchen-Table Dealers](https://www.vpc.org/studies/dealkit.htm), [Everytown — Inside the Gun Shop](https://everytownresearch.org/report/firearms-dealers-and-their-impact/), [Cabela's Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabela's), [Academy Sports Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Sports_+_Outdoors), profit-margin figures from [businessplan-templates.com](https://businessplan-templates.com/blogs/profits/gun-store) and [efinancialmodels.com](https://www.efinancialmodels.com/are-gun-stores-profitable-the-untold-truth-behind-their-success/) (industry ≈$14B annually, 20–30% overall shop margin, but only ~12% margin specifically on new-gun sales, with accessories/ammo up to 30% of revenue and higher-margin).

## 3. Pain Points by Segment

**ATF inspection / revocation risk (zero-tolerance era):**
- Revocations: **5 (H2 2021) → 88 (2022) → 157 (2023) → 147 (2024)** — highest revocation rate since 2005, >2% of all licenses inspected in the peak year. [The Trace](https://www.thetrace.org/2024/10/atf-gun-dealer-licenses-revoked-biden/), [The Truth About Guns](https://www.thetruthaboutguns.com/atf-ffl-revocations-under-biden/).
- Additional **247 voluntary surrenders** post-inspection reported by one source; another source cites **1,480+ surrenders** shutting down entirely rather than fight a hearing — figures vary by methodology/period, worth independent verification. [NSSF](https://www.nssf.org/articles/atfs-zero-tolerance-unrelenting-and-revealing/), [Everytown](https://www.everytown.org/press/new-atf-inspection-data-shows-the-biden-harris-administration-is-holding-rogue-gun-dealers-accountable/).
- FY2024: **9,696 firearm compliance inspections**. [Orchid Advisors](https://orchidadvisors.com/2024-top-atf-form-4473-errors-published-2025/).
- Policy note: zero-tolerance was instituted under Biden (June 2021); the Trump DOJ has moved to roll back related enforcement policy in 2025-26 (see Section 6).

**Most common Form 4473 violations:** #1 recurring violation for over a decade — buyer failing to properly complete Section A, especially Q10(a)/(b) ethnicity/race and Q12 non-citizenship items; recordkeeping errors like wrong model, transposed/missing serial numbers, misentered caliber; blank NTN/date fields on Q27 delayed/denied responses; use of outdated form revisions (only Aug 2023 revision valid as of Feb 1, 2024). [Orchid Advisors](https://orchidadvisors.com/2024-top-atf-form-4473-errors-published-2025/), [Orchid — 7 of 10 violations on 4473](https://orchidadvisors.com/7-out-of-10-compliance-violations-occur-on-atf-form-4473/).

**Paper vs. electronic bound books:** Adoption is partial and uneven — FastBound alone processed ~1.5M 4473s in 2024 (~10% of adjusted NICS volume); Orchid eBound processes ~25M transactions/year; but paper record-book suppliers (e.g., RK Enterprises, 18,000 customers) remain in active business, confirming a large unconverted paper base. One vendor's 2026 marketing claims "half of FFLs are failing ATF inspections" over paper/digital gaps (self-interested source, treat as directional not verified). [FastBound](https://www.fastbound.com/ffl-bound-book-software-features/), [Bravo/e4473 blog](https://www.bravostoresystems.com/post/paper-vs-digital-why-half-of-ffls-are-failing-atf-inspections-in-2026).

**NICS delays:** >90% resolve in minutes; ~10% need review, ~4% take 3+ business days (triggering default-proceed risk); in 2024 the FBI **failed to report final disposition on ~71% of delayed (3+ day) cases**; for the subset that resulted in a prohibited-person transfer under default-proceed, average **25 business days** elapsed before FBI flagged it as should-have-been-denied — a real liability exposure for dealers who sold under default-proceed. [GOA](https://www.gunowners.org/fbi-admits-it-is-often-wrong-on-gun-related-background-check-denials/), [FastBound NICS delay guide](https://www.fastbound.com/how-to-check-the-status-of-your-nics-delay/), [LocalFFL](https://localffl.com/guides/background-check-delays-faq).

**Payment processing discrimination:** ISO approved a dedicated firearms Merchant Category Code (MCC) in Sept 2022; Visa/Mastercard/Amex initially planned adoption, faced backlash from 24 GOP state AGs, paused in March 2023; California then legally mandated the code be applied to CA transactions, and card networks began complying with that state mandate in 2024 — creating a **patchwork of state-driven requirements** dealers must navigate, plus ongoing concern about singling out gun purchases for special tracking/scrutiny by banks. [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/politics/visa-mastercard-discover-pause-work-code-would-track-gun-purchases), [Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/state-laws-gun-merchant-category-code-visa-mastercard-amex-discover/695118/), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/07/01/credit-card-gun-sales-policy-flight-california-visa-mastercard-codes-gun-store-purchases/).

**Thin margins:** Industry-wide ~20–30% overall margin, but **firearms themselves carry only ~12% margin**; shops depend on **accessories/ammo/services (up to 30%+ of revenue)** and gunsmithing/training for profitability — meaning software that only tracks firearms compliance misses where the money is actually made. [businessplan-templates.com](https://businessplan-templates.com/blogs/profits/gun-store).

**Not independently verified this round** (flagged gaps): insurance cost/availability trends, adult-signature shipping rule specifics, distributor inventory management pain points.

## 4. Willingness to Pay

Direct forum/Reddit sentiment on switching costs wasn't surfaced in search results (a genuine research gap — worth a dedicated Reddit/forum scrape). Vendor pricing observed in market:
- FFL POS/compliance software starts around **$99–$200/month** at the low end; most vendors (FastBound, Orchid eBound, FFL-BRO, AIM POS, Bravo/e4473) use **custom quotes** once hardware, 4473 e-forms, and bound-book modules are bundled — advertised entry price rarely reflects final bill.
- Vendors emphasize migration support as a key sales point, implying **switching friction/fear is a real barrier** small shops weigh against any price difference.
- Sources: [Bravo — Best FFL POS Systems 2026](https://www.bravostoresystems.com/best-ffl-point-of-sale-systems-in-2026), [AIM POS](https://aim-pos.com/best-gun-store-pos-software/), [Capterra — FFL-BRO pricing](https://www.capterra.com/p/10036831/FFL-BRO/).

## 5. Adjacent Customers

- **Private-party sellers needing transfers** — a large, uncounted long-tail using local FFLs as pass-through; growth driver for range/shop transfer-fee revenue and a channel dependent on the now-contested "engaged in the business" line (see Section 6).
- **C&R collectors (Type 03)** — cheap ($30/3yr), no storefront/inventory reporting requirement; likely light/no-tech users, low near-term commercial priority.
- **Law-enforcement distributors** — specialized government/LE sales channel (not directly quantified here).
- **Importers/exporters (ITAR/EAR)** — Type 08 importers plus destructive-device Type 09/10/11 holders face additional State Department ITAR / Commerce EAR compliance layers on top of ATF; a natural high-complexity/high-willingness-to-pay compliance-software niche.

## 6. The 2024 "Engaged in the Business" Rule — Status

- **April 10, 2024:** ATF finalized the rule broadening who counts as "engaged in the business" as a dealer. [ATF final rule](https://www.atf.gov/rules-and-regulations/final-rule-definition-engaged-business-a-dealer-firearms).
- **May–July 2024:** Litigation filed — Texas AG Paxton (joined by LA, MS, UT) and separately the NRA (Butler v. Bondi). [The Reload](https://thereload.com/members-newsletter-whats-in-the-atf-gun-rules/), [NRA-ILA](https://www.nraila.org/articles/20251002/federal-court-strikes-down-biden-administration-s-engaged-in-the-business-rule-in-nra-case).
- **June 2025:** Federal judge in Texas issued a nationwide preliminary injunction; the Butler v. Bondi district court held ATF exceeded statutory authority. [Ammoland](https://www.ammoland.com/2025/10/judge-knocks-down-atfs-engaged-in-the-business-rule/), [Gun Tests](https://www.gun-tests.com/gun-tests-plus/federal-court-strikes-down-atfs-expanded-engaged-in-the-business-rule/).
- **April 24, 2026:** Trump DOJ filed to drop its appeal, letting the strike-down stand. [YourNews](https://yournews.com/2026/04/25/6851781/doj-drops-appeal-on-atf-engaged-in-the-business-rule/).
- **May 6, 2026:** A proposed rule to formally **rescind** the 2024 rule was issued, part of a broader "New Era of Reform" 34-rule reform package under Executive Order 14206. [ZeroHedge](https://www.zerohedge.com/political/major-victory-gun-owners-atf-unleashes-34-rule-reform-package-brace-rule-dead), [Orchid Advisors — Trump ATF Rule Changes](https://orchidadvisors.com/trump-atf-rule-changes-ffls/).

**Implication for platform sizing:** the rule is dead/being formally rescinded as of mid-2026. This **caps near-term growth in the number of people legally required to hold an FFL**. Total FFL count should be modeled as roughly flat-to-slowly-declining rather than assuming a new wave of forced licensees. Important negative revision to any TAM model built before mid-2025.

## Customer Insight: Where a New Platform Should Target First

**Recommendation: small-to-midsize independent retail gun shops and ranges-with-retail that already have a storefront and staff (not pure kitchen-table FFLs, not the big chains) — the "5–50 employee, single-to-few-location dealer" segment.**

Reasoning:
1. **Kitchen-table/home-based FFLs (~57% of licensed addresses)** are the largest segment by count but structurally low-revenue, price-sensitive, and often side-income — low willingness to pay.
2. **Large chains** are few in number, already running enterprise POS/ERP with dedicated compliance staff, and demand long enterprise sales cycles — hard first beachhead.
3. **The independent shop/range-with-retail segment** is where the pain concentrates and is monetizable: full ATF inspection/revocation exposure but **no dedicated compliance staff**, **still disproportionately on paper bound books**, **enough revenue to afford $100–300/month SaaS**, and **numerous enough (tens of thousands of storefronts)** for scalable GTM. They also run the diversified ammo/accessories/gunsmithing/range-fee revenue mix a good platform should unify.
4. With the "engaged in the business" rescission, **the addressable market is the existing ~46,000 Type 01 dealers plus overlapping Type 07/02 storefronts** — a stable, identifiable, reachable target list (ATF publishes the FFL list).
5. Secondary expansion: **regional multi-location chains** (between independents and Academy/Bass Pro) where compliance complexity multiplies across locations but enterprise lock-in is weaker.

**Deprioritize initially:** kitchen-table/C&R collectors and the largest chains. Type 07 manufacturers bifurcate into "small dealer-in-manufacturer-clothing" (same profile as independents) vs "true production manufacturer" (different software needs: production/serialization, not POS) — worth a follow-up pass before committing GTM there.

**Research gaps flagged for follow-up:** exact current Type 03 count, SOT Class 1/2/3 counts, insurance market specifics, adult-signature shipping rules, distributor inventory pain points, and direct forum sentiment on software switching costs.
